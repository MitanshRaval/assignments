1. Six Phases of SDLC
Requirement  –  Identify and understand what the customer or user needs from the software.
Design –        Plan the software structure, user interface, database, and overall system     design.
Development –   Programmers write the code and build the software according to the design.
Testing –       Test the software to find and fix bugs and make sure it works correctly.
Deployment –    Release the completed software so that users can use it.
Maintenance –    Monitor the software, fix problems, and add improvements or new features when needed.

3 Differences Between Agile and Waterfall Models

Agile	Waterfall

1. Agile development is done in small, repeated cycles called iterations or sprints.
2. Changes can be easily made during development based on feedback.	
3. Testing is performed continuously throughout development.	

waterfall

1.Waterfall follows a fixed sequence of phases, one after another.
2.Changes are difficult and costly once a phase is completed.
3.Testing usually takes place after the development phase is completed.

3 Main Scrum Roles

Product Owner (PO) – Manages the product requirements and decides what features should be developed.
Scrum Master – Helps the team follow Scrum practices and removes obstacles that affect the team's work.
Development Team – Designs, develops, tests, and delivers the working product or features.

4. What is a Sprint in Scrum?

A Sprint is a fixed period in Scrum during which the team works on a specific set of tasks and delivers a working part of the product. A typical Sprint lasts 1 to 4 weeks, with 2 weeks being very common.

5. Jira Board Columns

To Do – Tasks that are planned but have not been started yet.
In Progress – Tasks that the team is currently working on.
Done – Tasks that have been completed and meet the required conditions.
Backlog – A list of tasks, features, and requirements that are planned for future work.
